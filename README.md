# FUTZAV Poultry Sales Analysis | Power BI

**Turning Poultry Sales Data into Actionable Business Intelligence**

## Project Overview

This project analyses poultry sales and distribution performance to uncover patterns in product demand, revenue generation, profitability, customer acquisition and regional market opportunities.

Developed using **Microsoft Power BI**, the project transforms structured poultry sales data into an interactive business intelligence dashboard designed to support data-driven decision-making, improve sales performance and identify opportunities for profitable growth.

The analysis focuses on the operational and commercial questions that matter to a poultry distribution business: which products drive demand, where customers are located, which sales channels perform best, and how costs influence profitability.

## Business Problem

Poultry distribution businesses operate in a market where product demand, distribution costs, customer behaviour and delivery efficiency can influence overall performance.

The objective of this project is to use sales data to answer key business questions:

- Which poultry products generate the highest sales volume?
- How does revenue and profitability change over time?
- Which regions demonstrate stronger demand?
- Which customer acquisition channels contribute most to sales?
- How do procurement, transportation and handling costs affect profitability?
- What actions could improve sales performance and distribution efficiency?

## Project Objectives

- Evaluate sales volume and revenue performance across poultry product categories.
- Analyse profitability and cost components.
- Identify regional demand patterns and potential expansion opportunities.
- Examine sales channels and customer segments.
- Build an interactive dashboard that communicates findings clearly.
- Translate analytical findings into practical business recommendations.

## Key Performance Indicators

The dashboard focuses on core commercial metrics, including:

- **Total Revenue** — overall sales value.
- **Total Chicks Ordered** — total quantity demanded.
- **Total Net Profit** — profitability after recorded costs.
- **Product Performance** — sales volume and revenue by product category.
- **Regional Performance** — demand across geographical markets.
- **Sales Channel Performance** — contribution of different acquisition and sales channels.

## Analytical Approach

### 1. Sales Performance Analysis
Evaluated sales volume and revenue across poultry product categories to identify high-demand products and understand the distribution of sales.

### 2. Profitability and Cost Analysis
Examined revenue alongside procurement, transportation, handling and other recorded costs to understand their relationship with gross and net profit.

### 3. Regional Demand Analysis
Compared sales across geographical markets to identify areas with stronger demand and potential opportunities for distribution expansion.

### 4. Customer and Sales Channel Analysis
Investigated sales channels and customer segments to understand how demand is generated and where marketing or customer acquisition efforts could be prioritised.

### 5. Interactive Dashboard Development
Designed a Power BI dashboard with visual summaries and interactive filtering to support exploration by product type and order date.

## Business Recommendations

The analysis can support the following management decisions, subject to the findings in the dataset:

1. **Prioritise high-demand products:** Align procurement and inventory planning with demonstrated sales patterns.
2. **Improve distribution planning:** Consider regional demand, transportation costs and delivery requirements when allocating stock.
3. **Strengthen effective sales channels:** Direct marketing resources towards channels that demonstrate measurable commercial value.
4. **Monitor profitability:** Track net profit and distribution costs alongside revenue and sales volume.
5. **Use data for planning:** Review sales trends regularly to support forecasting, resource allocation and growth decisions.
